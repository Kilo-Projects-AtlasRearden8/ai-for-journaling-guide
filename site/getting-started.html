Getting Started with AI Journaling

AI journaling is simply using an AI assistant as a reflective companion. You write, think, or speak — and AI helps you dig deeper, spot patterns, and organize your inner world.

This page helps you understand the landscape and pick a path that fits your comfort level and privacy needs.

What AI Journaling Can Do

AI can support your journaling practice in several ways:

Capability How It Helps
Prompt generation Offers gentle, open-ended questions when you're stuck
Pattern recognition Notices recurring themes across your entries
Summarization Recaps your week or month in a few sentences
Mood tracking Helps you tag and visualize emotional trends
Organization Tags, categorizes, and retrieves entries by topic
Idea expansion Builds on half-formed thoughts without judgment

Choosing Your Approach

You don't need a fancy tool to start. Here are three tiers, from simplest to most private:

Tier 1: Cloud-Based AI (Fastest Start)

If you're comfortable with major AI providers and want convenience:

  • ChatGPT (free tier), Claude, or Gemini
  • Simply paste or type your journal entry
  • Ask follow-up questions like "What themes do you notice?" or "What might I explore further?"

Privacy tip: Never paste entries containing names, addresses, medical details,
or anything you wouldn't share with a stranger. Use a private browser window.

Tier 2: Local AI (More Privacy)

Run models on your own machine:

  • Ollama + models like Llama 3, Phi-3, or Mistral
  • LM Studio (Windows/macOS) with a simple GUI
  • Jan (open-source desktop app)
# Example with Ollama
ollama run llama3
# Then paste your journal entry and ask for reflection prompts

Tier 3: Self-Hosted (Maximum Privacy)

  • Run a model on a personal server or home network
  • Use tools like Text Generation Web UI, Ollama (server mode), or Ollama LLM
  • No internet required after initial model download

Your First Entry: A Template

  1. Set the scene — "Here's what happened today..."
  2. Name a feeling — "I felt..."
  3. Ask AI one question — "What's worth reflecting on here?"
  4. Respond in your own words — no need to be polished
  5. Save it — in a file or on paper

Quick Tips

  • Start with 2–3 minutes, not 20
  • It's okay to say "I don't know" or "Nothing much happened"
  • AI is a mirror, not a therapist — use discernment
  • Keep a local backup of your entries
