Mood Tracking — Gentle Patterns Over Time

You don't need a color-coded spreadsheet to track your mood. A few notes a day
can reveal patterns that help you understand yourself better.

The Simple Mood Log

Each day, note:

  1. Your mood (use words, not just numbers)
  2. One event or thought tied to that mood
  3. One thing you did that helped (or drained you)

Example:

October 20 — Tired but content. Walked in the park after lunch.
Energy dipped around 3 PM.

Word-Based Mood Tracking

Numbers can feel clinical. Try mood words instead:

Tier Words
High Energy Energized, inspired, playful, curious
Steady Grounded, content, calm, focused
Low Energy Tired, quiet, heavy, numb, restless
Challenging Anxious, frustrated, sad, overwhelmed, uncertain

Ask yourself: "What's the dominant word for today?"

AI-Enhanced Mood Reflection

Once a week, paste a few entries into your AI tool and ask:

  • "What patterns do you notice in my mood entries?"
  • "Do my energy levels correlate with any activities I mentioned?"
  • "What might I experiment with next week based on these entries?"

What Patterns Look Like

Don't overanalyze. Common mood patterns:

  • Energy dips after meetings or late nights
  • Better moods following walks, creative time, or quiet mornings
  • Consistency matters more than peaks — small, steady wins
  • Rest days are productive days, not failures

Low-Energy Friendly Tracking

If even daily check-ins feel like too much:

  • Track mood 2–3 times a week instead of every day
  • Use a single word: "Today felt: tired."
  • Let AI summarize: "In your last 10 entries, you mentioned 'tired' 4 times."

Privacy Note

Mood data is deeply personal. Keep it local, redact identifiers, and remember
that AI mood tools are reflective — not diagnostic.
